## 1. Cap backstop counts a shuffle re-key as new cards and silently drops the crown's confirm

- **Area / severity:** server / medium
- **Where:** `server/src/room.ts:520`
- **Breaks:** 8.7 / O43 (the cap is cards the table would hold; a re-key holds the count steady), 7.2 / O66 / O103(1) re-keying, 8.1 (every browser applies the crown's confirmed ops)
- **Scenario:** cardsAdded() (room.ts 67-74) adds up every `add` in a batch against the table as it was before the op, and never subtracts the batch's `remove`. When the crown shuffles hidden cards, afterShuffle (src/online/authority.ts 357) sends `confirm {by: crown, op: batch[remove N old uids, add N fresh uids]}` with no `id`. The server counts that as N cards added. Two ways to hit it: (a) a table of 4,950 cards and an S S of a 60-card face-down library, which is checked as 4,950 + 60 > 5,000; (b) any table taken online over the cap, which 8.7 allows ('goes online anyway'), and any re-key of 2 or more hidden cards. Line 520 then returns before seq++. With no id, nobody is told. The crown has already applied the re-key, so its cards carry new uids, while the mirror and every other browser keep the old ones. The crown's next 30 s snapshot overwrites the mirror with the new uids, but the other players never get a fresh table. From then on, their moves and draws of those cards name uids the crown doesn't have, and the crown's moves name uids they don't have. Tables diverge until each player reconnects.
- **Verifier:** server/src/room.ts:68-74: cardsAdded() on a batch adds up its children against the mirror as it was before the op. A `remove` counts 0, and an `add` counts every uid the mirror does not have. afterShuffle (src/online/authority.ts:351-360) builds batch[remove hidden, add fresh] with brand-new uids, applies it to the crown's own table with inbox.applyRemote, and sends `confirm {by: me, op}` with no id. It never goes through check()/checkOp, so the crown's own cap logic never sees it. On the server, confirm() at room.ts:520 counts N added, so mirror.cards.length + N > 5000 is true for a 4,950-card table and a 60-card face-down S S. It is also true for any re-key of 2 or more hidden cards on a table taken online over the cap, which 8.7 allows. The function then returns before seq++, and because `id` is undefined nobody is told. The crown keeps the new uids. The mirror and every other browser keep the old ones and see no seq gap (inbox.ts:181-182), so nothing asks for a snapshot. The crown's 30 s snapshot (snapshotPart, room.ts:622-653) replaces only the mirror and sends it to no one except `awaiting`. From then on, ops naming either set of uids miss on the other side, and the tables diverge until people reconnect. This breaks 8.7/O43 (a re-key keeps the count the same) and 7.2/O66/O103(1).
- **Fix suggested:** Make cardsAdded net out the batch's removals: for a batch, apply each child to a running copy, or subtract `remove` uids that the batch's `add`s replace. Better still, compare the card count after applyOp against the count before, and refuse only when it grows past MAX_CARDS. When there is no id, ask the crown for a snapshot instead of dropping the confirm silently.
- **Status:** open

## 2. Wrong-password limit lives only in memory and resets every time the room hibernates

- **Area / severity:** server / low
- **Where:** `server/src/room.ts:115`
- **Breaks:** 10.4 / O69 (10 wrong passwords per 10 minutes per ip, then bye rate for 10 minutes), O98(1)
- **Scenario:** passwordRate is an in-memory SlidingWindow on the Room, rebuilt empty whenever the constructor runs. Take a Public + password room listed in the browser whose players are idle. An attacker sends 10 hellos with wrong keys and gets `bye rate`. The room then has no sockets talking and no timers once the 1 s delays finish, so it hibernates about 10 s later. The next hello wakes it with an empty window and buys 10 more guesses. That is roughly 10 guesses every 12-15 s (tens of thousands a day) instead of 10 per 10 minutes. The join limit doesn't help, because it is only checked after the password matches. O105 chose in-memory rate windows for the Lobby only; nothing decides this for the Room's password window.
- **Verifier:** room.ts:115: passwordRate is a field initialised in the constructor, which reruns on every wake from hibernation, and nothing stores it. I confirmed this on the local server. A crown stayed idle in a room with a password set, and 10 wrong hellos each got `bye password`; the 11th got `bye rate`. After 20 s idle the log showed 'room HGD8AQ woke with 1 socket(s)', and guesses 12 to 14 got `bye password` again, not `bye rate`. So an attacker gets about 10 guesses for every hibernation cycle (roughly every 15-20 s) instead of 10 per 10 minutes. The Lobby join limit does not help, because hello() checks it only after the password matches (room.ts:292). O105 kept rate windows in memory for the Lobby only. 10.4/O69/O98(1) set 10 per 10 minutes per ip, then `bye rate` for 10 minutes. (A related point: allows() at line 282 runs before the awaited hash and hit() runs after it, so hellos sent in parallel also get past the count.)
- **Fix suggested:** Keep the wrong-password hits per ip in the Room's storage (one small KV entry pruned to the window), or route them through the Lobby as joins are. Count the hit before the await, for example hit() on entry and refund it on a correct key, so parallel hellos cannot all pass allows().
- **Status:** open

## 3. After the free plan refuses writes, a hibernation rolls the room back and seq goes backwards, so every client silently drops later ops

- **Area / severity:** server / low
- **Where:** `server/src/storage.ts:94`
- **Breaks:** O101(1) (a Room whose writes are refused carries on from memory rather than failing its players), 8.6 seq ordering, O49 ban for the room's life
- **Scenario:** After one quota-refused write, `refused = true` (line 99), and every later saveMeta, appendOps and writeSnapshot is skipped. That only works while the Room stays in memory. Once the room idles for about 10 s and hibernates, the constructor (room.ts 124-131) reloads the last saved meta and mirror, and `seq` drops back to the last logged value, for example 140, while every client has already applied up to 180. The next confirms are stamped 141, 142, and so on. Each client's inbox drops them at src/online/inbox.ts:181 (`msg.seq <= this.seq`), and its gap check only looks forward, so no snapshot is ever requested. The table stops syncing for everyone, with no message. Bans, password changes and seats made after the quota hit are also forgotten, so a banned player can come back.
- **Verifier:** storage.ts:94-100: after one quota error `refused` is set and every later saveMeta/appendOps/writeSnapshot is skipped, so the in-memory Room moves ahead of storage. Hibernation is real and frequent; my local test showed the constructor rerunning after about 20 s idle. On waking, the constructor (room.ts:124-131) builds a new RoomStorage (refused=false), loads the stale meta, and sets this.seq from the last stored snapshot plus the op log. That seq is lower than the seq clients have already applied. The next confirm() stamps seq+1. Inbox.op (src/online/inbox.ts:181) drops any msg.seq <= this.seq, and its gap check at line 182 only looks forward, so no needSnapshot is ever sent. Non-crown tables silently stop following until the server's seq passes theirs, and the ops in between are lost for good. Bans, seats and password changes made after the refusal also come back stale from KV, so a banned player is let back in by hello() (room.ts:278). This breaks O101(1) (carry on from memory rather than failing the players), the 8.6 seq ordering, and O49.
- **Fix suggested:** After a refused write, keep the Room awake or carry seq forward: for example, make wake() ask the crown for its snapshot and send every client a fresh table with the new seq. Simplest is to raise seq on wake to at least the highest seq any client reports, or to have clients treat a seq lower than their own as a reason to request a snapshot rather than drop it.
- **Status:** open

## 7. A 1,000-card snapshot part can pass the 256 KB part limit, and the server then throws the crown out with bye size

- **Area / severity:** shared / high
- **Where:** `shared/protocol.ts:37`
- **Breaks:** 8.7 (snapshot parts within the limits; the 110-bytes-a-card estimate is far off), 10.5 limits, 4.5 (the crown must stay)
- **Scenario:** splitSnapshot puts 1,000 cards in each part, and the server refuses any 'snapshot' message over MAX_SNAPSHOT_PART_BYTES (256 KB) with bye 'size' (room.ts:191). Close code 4000 follows, so the crown does not reconnect. Real CardSnaps are much bigger than the test's (tests/protocol.test.ts uses uid 'c1' and integer coordinates). Measured with the app's own uid format and full-precision floats: about 234 KB for plain cards, about 270 to 283 KB for 1,000 cards laid by an X spread (laidAt duplicates x and y, and spreadOf is set), and about 270 KB for cards on stands that were moved from the far side. Scenario: a table of 1,000 or more cards where a cube was spawned and X-spread. Go online triggers needSnapshot, and Authority.snapshot sends part 1 at over 256 KB, so the maker is kicked at once and the room never gets its table. In a running room the 30-second snapshot does the same, and each crown elected next is kicked in turn on its own next snapshot.
- **Verifier:** splitSnapshot (shared/protocol.ts 144-156) cuts parts by count only, SNAPSHOT_PART_CARDS = 1000, and Authority.snapshot (authority.ts 80-85) sends them as they are. The server's webSocketMessage (room.ts 190-191) applies maxBytes('snapshot') = 256 KB and calls bye(ws, 'size'), which closes with 4000. session.ts 239-240 does not reconnect after that. With side or spreadOf it is about 288 KB, and with stand and side about 268 KB. All of these are over 262,144 bytes. So when a run of about 800 or more X-spread cards falls in one 1,000-card slice, Go online (needSnapshot) or the 30-second snapshot kicks the crown. Each crown elected after that is kicked on its own next snapshot. This breaks 8.7 and 10.5 (parts within 256 KB) and 4.5.
- **Fix suggested:** Split snapshot parts by encoded size as well as count. For example, in splitSnapshot start a new part when the running byteLength of the part would pass a margin under MAX_SNAPSHOT_PART_BYTES (such as 200 KB), and keep the 32-part cap in mind. Or lower SNAPSHOT_PART_CARDS to about 500.
- **Status:** fixed by the hub: `splitSnapshot` closes a part at 1,000 cards or 200 KB of cards, whichever first (SNAPSHOT_PART_BUDGET); tested with 5,000 heavy cards.
